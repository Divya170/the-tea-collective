01 · Discover

Five ways to know tea.

General guideThese are general descriptions of each tea style, not details of The Tea Collective’s current inventory. Product-specific notes appear in the collection once supplied.

  1. Photo placeholder · Green tea leaves, close up

    01

    Green Tea

    Leaves are heated soon after picking, so they keep a fresh, green character.

    Typical character
    Grassy · vegetal · sometimes toasty or sweet
    Explore Green Tea → How to brew it

  2. Photo placeholder · Dark black tea leaves in a scoop

    02

    Black Tea

    Leaves are fully oxidised before drying, giving a darker cup and a fuller body.

    Typical character
    Malty · robust · sometimes fruity or smoky
    Explore Black Tea → How to brew it

  3. Photo placeholder · Silvery white tea buds

    03

    White Tea

    Young buds and leaves are simply withered and dried, with very light processing.

    Typical character
    Delicate · soft · lightly honeyed
    Explore White Tea → How to brew it

  4. Photo placeholder · Dried flowers and herbs

    04

    Herbal Tea

    Infusions of flowers, herbs, fruits or roots rather than the tea plant, and usually naturally caffeine-free.

    Typical character
    Floral · fruity · minty · spiced, depending on the ingredients
    Explore Herbal Tea → How to brew it

  5. Photo placeholder · A blend of leaves and botanicals

    05

    Specialty Blends

    A base tea combined with botanicals, spices or flavourings for a particular character.

    Typical character
    Varies with the base tea and what it’s blended with
    Explore Specialty Blends → How to brew it

02 · Brewing guide

Five steps to a good cup.

Simple habits that suit almost any tea. The guide below suggests temperatures and times for each style.

  1. 1

    Choose your tea

    Start with the tea you’re in the mood for: bright and fresh, rich and warming, or calm and floral.

  2. 2

    Prepare fresh water

    Fresh, cold water, filtered if your tap water is hard, gives the clearest flavour.

  3. 3

    Check the temperature

    Delicate teas prefer water below boiling; robust teas and herbals usually like it hotter.

  4. 4

    Allow the tea to steep

    Steep, then taste. Longer brings strength, and sometimes bitterness.

  5. 5

    Enjoy your cup

    Remove the leaves, take a moment, and notice how the cup tastes as it cools.

04 · The ritual

Steam, silence, and a second cup.

Warm the teapot. Watch the leaves open. Let the steam rise for a moment before the first sip. The ritual is as much a part of tea as the leaves themselves.
